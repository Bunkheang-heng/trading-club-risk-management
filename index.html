The Foundation

What Is Risk Management?

The Simple Truth of Trading Core Reality

No trader, algorithm, or mentor knows 100% where the next candlestick will go. Every single trade is simply a probability.

🚗 The Car Seatbelt Rule:
You don't wear a seatbelt expecting to crash. You wear it so a sudden mistake doesn't end your journey. In trading, Risk Management is your seatbelt.
What You Control: You cannot control the market. You can 100% control how much money you lose if you are wrong.
❌ The Beginner (Gambler) 90% Fail Here
Focuses on Greed: "How much money can I make?"
  • ✕ Bets random large lot sizes without calculation
  • ✕ Ignores stop loss; hopes the market comes back
  • ✕ 1 or 2 bad trades wipe out their entire account
✅ The Smart Trader (Risk Officer) Long-Term Winner
Focuses on Defense: "How much could I lose if I'm wrong?"
  • ✓ Always caps risk at 1% of account balance
  • ✓ Accepts small losses as a normal business expense
  • ✓ Stays calm, survives bad days, and trades tomorrow
Golden Rule of Survival: Your #1 job as a trader is not to get rich today — it is to protect your money so you can trade again tomorrow.

Step 01 • The Golden Rule

The 1% - 2% Golden Risk Rule

Surviving Losing Streaks Protection Law

In financial markets, even profitable hedge funds experience 5 to 7 losses in a row due to normal market randomness.

The 1% Rule Equation
Max Risk = Account Balance × 1%
On a $1,000 account, you risk exactly $10 per trade. Never more!

When you cap risk at 1%, a 5-loss streak is just a tiny scrape (-$50). But at 10%, that same streak cuts your entire account in half!

Step 03 • Trade Anatomy

Stop Loss & Take Profit on the Chart

Long Trade Execution: 1R Risk vs 3R Take Profit
TP: Target Liquidity Pool
Entry: POI Confirmation
SL: Structural Invalidation
The 3 Invariable Milestones Execution
  • 1
    1. Technical Invalidation (Stop Loss): Placed strictly behind market structure (swing low or order block). If this price level breaks, your trade hypothesis is dead.
  • 2
    2. Entry Trigger: Occurs only after price reaches your POI and confirms with a rejection wick or lower timeframe shift.
  • 3
    3. Objective Target (Take Profit): Anchored at opposing liquidity pools (equal highs, previous swing highs), yielding a minimum of 1:2.5 or 1:3 RRR.
Rule: Never enter a trade first, then search where to put your stop loss. The stop loss comes first.

Step 04 • Position Sizing

How to Pick Your Lot Size

The Master Lot Size Formula
Lot Size = &frac{Account Balance × Risk %}{Stop Loss (Pips) × Pip Value}
• Standard Forex: 1.00 Lot = $10 per pip on EUR/USD
• Micro Forex: 0.01 Lot = $0.10 per pip
Step-by-Step Practical Example EUR/USD Setup
  • 1. Account Balance: $1,000 | Risk Cap: 1% ($10 risk)
  • 2. Technical Invalidation: 20 pips away from Entry
  • 3. Pip Value per Lot: $10.00 / pip for 1.00 standard lot
  • 4. Calculation:
    $\text{Lot Size} = \frac{\$10}{20 \times \$10} = \frac{\$10}{\$200} = \mathbf{0.05 \text{ Lots}}$

Mentee Discipline

4 Golden Rules of Risk Management

01
Never Move Your Stop Loss Further

Widening a stop loss because "it will come back" is pure hopium. Once price reaches invalidation, the setup is dead. Take the small loss like a professional and move on.

02
Maximum Daily Loss Limit (2 Losses)

If you lose 2 trades in a single day (-2%), close your trading platform immediately. Go outside, study charts, or rest. Never fight an unfavorable market session.

03
Risk Is Defined Before Entry, Never After

Before pressing Buy or Sell, you must already know the exact Dollar risk, Lot size, Invalidation point, and Take Profit target. Zero surprises allowed.

04
Leverage Is a Tool, Not Capital

High broker leverage (1:100, 1:500) magnifies human emotion and accelerates account liquidation. Position sizing makes leverage irrelevant because risk is fixed at 1%.

Mentor Mantra: "Professional traders are professional risk managers who happen to trade."
